Predictive Models & Sources

This simulator implements established sports science models adapted for educational and informational use. The Riegel Power-Law formula is based on Pete Riegel's 1977 empirical research published in American Scientist. The Critical Speed model derives from foundational work by Hill (1925) and subsequent developments by Monod & Scherrer (1965) and Morton (1996). The MSS/MAS Blend formula references methodology described in Loporto & Mannion (2021), Aerobic and Anaerobic Speed Predicts 800-m Running Performance in Young Recreational Runners (PMC8176219). The VDOT model is based on Jack Daniels' oxygen-cost equivalent-performance framework as described in Daniels' Running Formula (1998, 2005), using the VO₂max/running-economy formula originally developed by Daniels & Gilbert. Ensemble weighting methodology is adapted from general principles of model averaging in predictive analytics and is not derived from any single proprietary source.

Accuracy & Limitations

Predicted times are statistical estimates for outdoor track and field with inherent uncertainty. Typical model error for well-trained athletes is approximately ±2–5 seconds for the 800m; errors may be larger for athletes whose profiles fall outside the collegiate-to-elite range on which these models were validated. Sex-adjustment coefficients are population-level averages and may not reflect individual variation. No warranty, express or implied, is made regarding the accuracy of any prediction. Results should not be used as the sole basis for race entry standards, scholarship decisions, medical clearance, or any consequential determination.
